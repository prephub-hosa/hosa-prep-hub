How HOSA Works

The Organization & Competitions

What is HOSA?

The Organization

HOSA – Future Health Professionals is a national career-technical student organization for health science students. With 300,000+ members and chapters in most US high schools and colleges, it's one of the largest pre-health pipelines in the country.

300,000+ members · All 50 states · High school & collegiate
Pick Your Event

60+ Competitive Events

Each member competes in one or more events from five categories — Health Science, Health Professions, Leadership, Recognition, and Service. The knowledge-based events on this site (Medical Terminology, Pathophysiology, Pharmacology, and more) are all written exam competitions.

Knowledge · Skills · Prepared · Leadership · Recognition
The Exam Format

100 Questions, 60 Minutes

Written knowledge events are 100 multiple-choice questions completed in 60 minutes. Questions test your event's specific content scope — so focused preparation on the right terms and concepts matters far more than general studying.

100 questions · 60-minute time limit · Multiple choice
Compete to Advance

Chapter → State → Nationals

You compete at your chapter or area conference first, then the top scorers advance to the State Leadership Conference. State winners earn a spot at the National Leadership Conference (NLC) held each June — where medals and scholarships are awarded.

Chapter → Area → State → NLC · June nationals

Competition Strategy.

📋
Start with the official test plan
HOSA publishes a detailed test plan for every event listing exact topic areas and their percentage weight. Every question on the exam comes from this plan — study the plan, not just random content.
🔗
Stack events for shared vocabulary
Medical Terminology → Anatomy & Physiology → Pathophysiology is the strongest stack. Each event reinforces the previous one. If you're doing Pharmacology, pair it with Pathophysiology — diseases and drugs share the same vocabulary.
⏱
Practice the pace, not just the content
100 questions in 60 minutes is 36 seconds per question. Students who can't maintain pace leave points on the table. Use Timed Test mode regularly — even if you know the content, racing the clock is a separate skill.
🔁
Review misses immediately, every time
After every quiz, read every wrong answer before you do anything else. The emotional sting of a wrong answer is your brain's fastest encoding trigger. Re-reading the right answer immediately is 3–4x more effective than reviewing it the next day.
📅
Build the daily habit, not the cram session
15 focused minutes every day for 8 weeks beats 6-hour cramming the week before. The SM-2 algorithm is designed for distributed practice — it only works if you show up daily. Set a study goal, track your streak, protect it.
🎯
Know your advancement target
Top 15% of scorers typically advance from area to state; top 10–15% advance from state to NLC. That usually means a score of 85–88+ for most events. Track your timed test scores in Your Library and trend them week over week.

Frequently Asked Questions.

How many events can I compete in?+
Most HOSA conferences allow members to compete in up to two competitive events. Check your state or chapter guidelines — some area conferences limit to one. Strategy tip: pick one knowledge event you're strong in and one you want to develop for the long run.
What score do I need to advance?+
It depends on your state and the year's competition. Generally: top 15% of scorers advance from area/chapter to state, and top 10–15% advance from state to NLC. In practice, that's usually 83–90+ for most written events. Some states are more competitive than others — aim for 88+ and you'll be safe in most states.
Does this site cover everything on the HOSA exam?+
HOSA Prep Hub covers the key terms, concepts, and clinical knowledge from each event's official test plan. The flashcards and quizzes target the highest-yield content — the stuff most likely to appear on your exam. That said, no prep site is exhaustive. Review the official HOSA test plan for your event and cross-reference with what you've studied here.
How long should I prepare?+
8–12 weeks of consistent daily study (15–20 min/day) is solid preparation for most events. 3+ months is ideal. If you're starting 2 weeks out, don't panic — switch to intensive mode: do two Timed Tests per day, review every miss, and drill your weakest cards. You can still make significant gains in two weeks.
What's the difference between area, state, and NLC?+
HOSA competition runs in three tiers. (1) Chapter or Area Conference: your first competition, usually held November–February. Top scorers advance. (2) State Leadership Conference (SLC): state-level competition, usually February–April. Top scorers advance. (3) National Leadership Conference (NLC): held each June, this is the national championship. Medalists and scholarship recipients are recognized here.
What is spaced repetition and why does it matter?+
Spaced repetition is a study method where you review material at increasing intervals over time — just before you'd normally forget it. HOSA Prep Hub uses the SM-2 algorithm (the same one used by Anki, the leading memory tool). When you rate a card "Know it," the algorithm shows it again in 4 days, then 10, then 25, etc. Cards you miss get shown sooner. This is why daily study beats cramming — the algorithm can only work if you come back.
Which event should I pick as a beginner?+
Start with Medical Terminology. It's the vocabulary foundation for every other event, it has a well-defined test plan, and it's the most beginner-accessible knowledge event. If you're interested in clinical medicine, pair it with Anatomy & Physiology or Pathophysiology as a second event. Events like Pharmacology or Biochemistry have higher ceilings and reward deeper preparation.
Is this site free? Do I need an account?+
Completely free. You can study all 70 events without creating an account — your progress saves to your browser's local storage automatically. Creating an account (also free) lets you sync your progress across devices through Firebase. No subscription, no paywall.

Clinical Communication Tips

🤝
Use "usted," not "tú"
Always use the formal "usted" register with patients. This is the clinical standard across all Spanish-speaking regions and shows professional respect.
🔊
Speak slowly and clearly
Speak at a moderate pace and avoid idioms. Many patients may have limited health literacy — direct, simple language protects patient safety.
✅
Confirm understanding
Always verify comprehension with "¿Me entiende?" and ask the patient to repeat instructions back. Don't rely on a nod — confirm with words.
⚠️
Know your limits
For complex medical discussions, use a certified medical interpreter. These phrases are for initial patient comfort — not a substitute for professional interpretation in critical situations.
